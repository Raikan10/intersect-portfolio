Many owners assume they need new software before AI is any use to them. Accounts in one system, everything else in spreadsheets, customers talking to the team over chat. It feels too messy to build on.

It usually is not.

## Start with the questions owners already ask

Every week, someone in the business compiles the same few reports by hand. Sales by customer and product. Overdue payments by age. Stock that has not moved. Progress against targets.

Those reports are the place to start, because the questions are already known and the data already exists. The work is:

1. Bring the numbers from the core system and the important spreadsheets into one place, on a schedule.
2. Build the drill-downs owners actually use: plant to product to customer, or region to dealer to invoice.
3. Let people ask questions in plain language, so someone can ask "which customers are past 45 days" from their phone.

For one manufacturer on a decades-old system, the first working version of this ran six days after the first meeting. You can read that [case study](/work/manufacturer-sales-mis/).

## Then add alerts

Once the numbers are in one place, the next step is not more dashboards. It is alerts: a short daily message listing what needs attention. Payments crossing a threshold, a large customer who has not ordered this month, stock about to run out.

Alerts work because nobody has to remember to look.

## Then remove the re-typing

The last step is automating the copying between systems. Orders that arrive in a chat and get typed into the accounts. Numbers that get pasted from one system into another every morning. These are standard, repetitive and error-prone, which makes them good candidates. Anything unusual goes to a person.

## What to check first

Before any of this, make sure the numbers reconcile. A report that disagrees with the books will not be trusted, and a system nobody trusts does not get used.
