Answer each question yes or no. There is no score. The point is to see where you would get stuck.

## The work

1. **Is there a task your team does the same way every week?** Answering the same queries, making the same report, entering the same data.
2. **Could you describe that task in writing in one page?** If it only lives in one person's head, writing it down is step one.
3. **Do you know roughly how many hours it takes?** Even a guess is enough to estimate payback.

## The data

4. **Does the data for that task exist somewhere?** Your accounts, spreadsheets, email and chat threads all count.
5. **Do the numbers in different places agree?** If the sales report and the books disagree, people will not trust anything built on top.
6. **Can you get to the data without asking a vendor's permission?** Some legacy systems make this the hardest step.

## The people

7. **Is there one person who owns the outcome?** Not the IT person. The person who feels the pain when it goes wrong.
8. **Will that person spend a few hours explaining how it really works?** The real process is never the one in the manual.
9. **Is there a decision-maker who will say yes to a fixed-price build?** Pilots that need five approvals tend to stall.

## The expectations

10. **Would you be happy if AI handled the standard 80% and a person kept the rest?** Projects that aim for 100% automation on day one usually ship nothing.

## Reading your answers

Mostly yes on the work and data questions means you have a good first project. A no on question 7 is the most common blocker, and no technology fixes it.
