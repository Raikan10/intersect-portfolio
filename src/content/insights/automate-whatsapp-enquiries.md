WhatsApp is where a lot of Indian business actually happens. Dealers ask for prices in groups, customers send order photos, suppliers confirm dispatch. So "can we automate WhatsApp" is one of the most common questions we hear.

The honest answer is: measure first.

## What we measured

For one electronics distributor with around 850 dealers, we read every message in two dealer WhatsApp groups over 110 days, 510 messages in all, and grouped them into query threads.

- **96% of dealer threads** opened with "price of model X" or "price and stock".
- **The median reply time was 3 minutes.** The team was already fast in the typical case.
- **12% of threads** were never answered, answered only after the dealer asked again, or got "will revert" and nothing after.
- Most of the misses were laptops described by specification, not printers asked for by model number.

## What that meant

A bot would have replaced the part that was already working. Dealers trust a named salesperson, and the speed was good most of the time. The money was in the 12%.

So the recommendation was a copilot for the salesperson: draft price and stock replies for a person to check and send, and alert when a thread has gone quiet. Full details are in the [case study](/work/dealer-whatsapp-copilot/).

## The prerequisite nobody wants to hear

Any automation on WhatsApp is only as good as the data behind it. Without a clean daily price list, current stock status and a product catalogue that matches how customers describe things, a bot will answer confidently and wrongly.

That data work is not glamorous, but it is the part that makes everything after it possible.

## A simple way to start

Export a month of your WhatsApp business chats. Count what people ask, how long replies take, and how many questions are never answered. That one exercise will tell you whether you need a bot, a copilot, or just a better price list.
