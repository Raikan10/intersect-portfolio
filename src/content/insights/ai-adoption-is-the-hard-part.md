The demo almost always works. A model reads the invoices, answers the questions, drafts the replies. Everyone in the room is impressed.

Three months later, the team is doing it the old way.

## Why it happens

The tool was added to the work. The work itself did not change.

Approvals still go through the same person. The report still gets made by hand because the owner does not quite trust the new one. The salesperson still types replies because nobody told them the drafts are now the default.

We have seen this in our own businesses. Giving a team better AI tools did not change output by itself. What changed output was deciding who owns which data, who approves what, and where work is allowed to wait.

## What the research says

Studies of companies that scale AI keep finding the same split: the technology is the smaller part of the effort. Most of it goes into changing roles, decision rights, training and how work is reviewed. One widely used rule of thumb puts it at 10% technology and 90% adoption.

There is a second effect worth knowing. AI makes routine work cheaper, so the human job shifts from doing the work to deciding and checking. That needs different skills, and it needs someone to design the review step on purpose.

## What to do about it

- **Name an owner.** One person who feels it when the process goes wrong, not the IT team.
- **Change the default.** The new way should be the easiest way. If the old spreadsheet still exists, people will use it.
- **Write down the exceptions.** Decide in advance which cases go to a person, so nobody has to guess.
- **Measure hours, not features.** Hours returned and errors avoided are what tell you the project worked.
- **Budget for the rollout.** Plan for training and a few weeks of support after go-live, not just the build.

That is why every [engagement](/services/) we run includes the rollout, and why we stay until the team uses it without us.
