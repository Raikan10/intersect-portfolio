A large share of Indian businesses with 20 to 500 people run on Tally for accounts, Excel for everything Tally does not do, and WhatsApp for talking to customers and suppliers. Owners often assume they need a new ERP before AI is any use to them.

They usually do not.

## Start with the questions owners already ask

Every week, someone in the business compiles the same few reports by hand. Sales by customer and product. Overdue payments by age. Stock that has not moved. Scheme or target progress.

Those reports are the place to start, because the questions are already known and the data already exists. The work is:

1. Pull data from Tally and the important spreadsheets into one database on a schedule.
2. Build the drill-downs owners actually use: unit to product to customer, or region to dealer to invoice.
3. Put a plain-language chat on top, so someone can ask "which customers are past 45 days" from their phone.

For one manufacturer on a legacy system, the first working version of this ran six days after the first meeting. You can read that [case study](/work/manufacturer-sales-mis/).

## Then add alerts

Once the numbers are in one place, the next step is not more dashboards. It is alerts: a short daily message listing what needs attention. Payments crossing a threshold, a large customer who has not ordered this month, stock about to run out.

Alerts work because nobody has to remember to look.

## Then remove the re-typing

The last step is automating the copying between systems. Orders that arrive on WhatsApp and get typed into Tally. Tally numbers that get pasted into brand portals. These are standard, repetitive and error-prone, which makes them good candidates. Anything unusual goes to a person.

## What to check first

Before any of this, make sure the numbers reconcile. A reporting layer that disagrees with Tally will not be trusted, and a system nobody trusts does not get used.
